How to Use Muster

The hall pass app. Everything you need, in order.

1. Signing in
2. Getting your students in
3. Writing a pass — computer
4. Writing a pass — phone
5. Logging them back in

The one-sentence version: every student has a QR code. Scan it (or tap their name), pick where they're going, tap the blue button. Scan again when they come back.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
1. Signing in
  1. Open the Muster link on your computer or phone.
  2. Tap the red Sign in with Google button.
  3. Pick your school Google account — the same one you use for school email.
  4. That's it. You land on your roster page.
  • You only have to do this once per device. It remembers you for about a month.
  • Your very first sign-in is what creates your account, so nothing will look set up until you've done it.
  • Sign out is in the top-right, but you usually don't need it. Leave yourself signed in on your classroom computer.
If it says you're not authorized: your email hasn't been added to the approved list yet, or you signed in with a personal Gmail instead of your school account. Check which account you used first, then ask Ms. Howe.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
2. Getting your students in

Two ways. Both live on the Roster page at the top.

One student at a time

  1. Tap + Add Student.
  2. Type first name, last name, pick a grade.
  3. Pick the class period they're in.
  4. Tap Add to My Roster.

A whole class at once

  1. Tap ⬆ Upload CSV.
  2. Choose the class period first — everyone in the file goes into that period.
  3. Choose your file, tap Upload.
  4. You'll get a little report: how many were added, how many were already there.

Your file needs exactly three columns, spelled like this:

first_name,last_name,grade
Jane,Smith,9
John,Doe,11
  • Grades must be 7 through 12.
  • Use this year's grade, not last year's. A 9th grader who's now in 10th needs to say 10.
  • Upload one file per class period. Six classes = six uploads.
Got a spreadsheet instead of a CSV? In Excel or Google Sheets choose File → Download / Save As → CSV. Make sure the very first row is first_name,last_name,grade.

Fixing your roster

  • The buttons across the top of your roster are your class periods. Tap one to see just that class. The number on each is how many students are in it.
  • Use the 🔍 search box to find one student fast.
  • Remove takes a student out of that one class. If you have them twice (two different periods), their other class isn't touched.
  • Put a whole class in the wrong period? Tap that period, then 🧹 Clear this period off my roster, then upload the file again under the right period.
Clearing or removing never deletes a student or their QR code. Their printed card keeps working, and their history is kept. You're only changing who's on your class list.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
3. Writing a pass — on your computer
  1. Your roster is the front page. Find the student.
  2. Tap the Log OUT button on their row.
  3. Pick where they're going:
    • Restroom
    • Nurse — opens boxes to check off what's wrong and what you already tried (water, bandaid, head down…). Check what applies; you don't have to fill in everything.
    • Office
    • Student Services — pick who they're seeing from the list, or pick Other and type a name.
    • Late Departure — type who's releasing them.
    • Going to Another Teacher — type which teacher.
  4. Tap the big blue Log Pass OUT →.
  • Their row now says OUT and starts counting minutes.
  • A banner at the top tells you how many of your students are out right now.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
4. Writing a pass — on your phone

Same thing, but you scan the student's QR card instead of hunting for their name.

  1. Sign in to Muster on your phone first. Do this once, before you ever scan.
  2. Open your phone's regular Camera app and point it at the student's QR card.
  3. Tap the link that pops up.
  4. Pick the pass type, tap Log Pass OUT →. Done.
Two things that trip people up:
  • Use the built-in Camera app. Downloaded "QR scanner" apps open the page inside their own little browser, which can't sign you in to Google. You'll get stuck on a login screen that won't work.
  • Sign in before you scan, not after. If you scan first you may just get bounced to a sign-in page.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
5. Logging them back in
  • On the computer: their row now shows a green Log IN. Tap it, then tap ✅ Log Back In.
  • On your phone: scan the same card again. Muster already knows they're out, so it just offers the green ✅ Log Back In button.
You never pick "in" or "out" — scanning the same card does whichever one makes sense. Out if they're in the room, in if they're already out.
  • Forgot to log someone back in? Just do it when you notice. The minutes will look long; that's fine, nobody's in trouble.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
6. Fire drill or lockdown

This is the part worth practicing before you need it.

Students on your roster

  1. Tap 🚨 My Roll Call at the top.
  2. It already shows the class you have right now.
  3. Tap Secure beside each student who is with you.
  4. Their row turns green and the count at the top goes up. The office sees it instantly.
  • Keep tapping — the page doesn't reload and won't lose your place.
  • If a button turns yellow and says Retry, tap it again. That's a weak signal, not a mistake you made.

A student who isn't yours

  1. Scan their QR card with your phone.
  2. Tap the big red 🚨 Mark ___ SECURE with me at the top of the page.
You can scan any student in the school, not just your own. That's on purpose — during a drill kids end up in the wrong room.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
7. Printing student cards

Every student needs their QR code on something. Tap Print at the top.

  • Lanyard cards — big cards, one per student. Laminate them.
  • 🏷 ID Stickers — small, about 32 to a page. Stick them on student IDs.
  1. Tap a class period first if you only want that class.
  2. Wait for every QR square to finish loading. If you print too fast you get blank boxes.
  3. Tap 🖨 Print.
  • Print once and you're done — a student's code never changes, all year.
  • Lost card? Reprint that class; it comes out the same.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
8. Warnings you might see

Muster shows these for you to use your judgment on. It never blocks a student on its own.

  • ⚠️ Used a pass in the last hour — they just went somewhere.
  • ⚠️ 3 passes today (with a red number by their name) — going a lot today.
  • 🔁 2nd day in a row — leaving the same class two days running. Sometimes that's a pattern, sometimes it's a stomach bug.
  • 🚧 Too many students in the hallway — the school-wide restroom count is at its cap. Wait for it to drop before sending another one.
  • 🚫 Cannot leave without an adult escort — an administrator has flagged this student, and there may be a note explaining why. The pass options disappear. Don't send them; call the office.
{# ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────── #}
9. When something looks wrong
  • My roster is empty. You haven't added students yet — see section 2.
  • A student's card won't scan. Check the QR is clean and not glare-covered, then look them up on your roster and tap Scan instead. Same screen, no camera needed.
  • Scanning sends me to a login page over and over. You're in a QR-scanner app's browser. Close it, open Muster in Safari or Chrome, sign in, then scan with the normal Camera app.
  • A student shows OUT but they're sitting right here. Log them back in — someone forgot. No harm done.
  • I sent someone out from the wrong period. Leave it. The time is recorded correctly; the period label is the only thing that's off.
  • A student is missing from My Roll Call. They're probably filed under a different period. Tap the other period buttons at the top.
  • A name is spelled wrong, or a student left the school. Ask Ms. Howe — those are admin jobs.
Still stuck? Tell Ms. Howe two things: what you tapped, and what it said back. That's usually enough to sort it out in a minute.
